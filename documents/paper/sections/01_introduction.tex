Vision impairment affects over 2.2 billion people globally, mainly due to cataracts, diabetic retinopathy, and glaucoma \cite{WHO2023Blindness}. Cataracts cause 45\% of global blindness (15.2 million people) \cite{GBD2024Cataract}, diabetic retinopathy is projected to reach 160.50 million cases by 2045 \cite{Pan2025DR}, and glaucoma, the leading cause of irreversible blindness, is expected to affect 111.8 million people by 2040 \cite{Vania2024Glaucoma}. These figures underline the urgent need for scalable early detection, especially in low- and middle-income regions with limited eye care access.

Deep learning, particularly Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) and Vision Transformers (ViTs), has become foundational in medical image analysis by extracting features from ophthalmic images without manual engineering \cite{Elkholy2024OCT}. ConvNeXt modernizes CNNs with Transformer design principles to improve accuracy while preserving efficiency \cite{Liu2022}, whereas DeiT uses knowledge distillation to train ViTs effectively on smaller datasets at lower computational cost \cite{touvron2021trainingdataefficientimagetransformers}.

Despite their high accuracy, deep learning models remain black boxes whose decision-making is difficult for humans to interpret \cite{Gomez2022Saliency}. Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) addresses this by visualizing the reasoning behind predictions: Grad-CAM produces importance heatmaps for CNNs such as ConvNeXt, while Attention Rollout or Gradient Attention Rollout aggregates layer-wise attention weights in Transformers such as DeiT \cite{Cho2025GMAR}. Because these mechanisms differ, their visualizations are not directly comparable, yet most medical deep learning evaluations stop at accuracy. Faithfulness can instead be quantified with deletion and insertion metrics, which progressively remove or reveal image regions by importance and measure the resulting change in model confidence as an Area Under the Curve (AUC) \cite{Gomez2022Saliency}.

Accordingly, this research has two analytical focuses. First, it compares the classification performance of ConvNeXt and DeiT on a four-class eye disease dataset (cataract, diabetic retinopathy, glaucoma, and normal) using accuracy, precision, recall, and F1-score. Second, it compares the faithfulness of Grad-CAM on ConvNeXt and Attention Rollout on DeiT using deletion and insertion AUC.

This research offers two contributions. Theoretically, it enriches the literature on the comparative interpretability of modern CNN (ConvNeXt) and ViT (DeiT) architectures in ophthalmic imaging by evaluating models on the faithfulness of their XAI visualizations rather than accuracy alone, an aspect rarely explored in previous studies. Practically, it provides empirical guidance for clinicians and engineers in choosing the architecture--XAI combination best suited to operational systems, particularly when a diagnostic system requires heatmaps closely aligned with the model's decision basis.
